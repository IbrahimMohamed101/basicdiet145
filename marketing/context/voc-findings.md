## Observed customer language

Short phrases retained because they are useful copy signals:
- "without feeling guilty"
- "clean & so tasty"
- reviewer described trying meals, then making a membership/subscription.

Do not quote customer names in marketing documents unless explicitly approved for testimonial use.

## Synthesized themes

### 1. Taste can be the lead message
Customers are not only commenting on "healthy" attributes; taste is directly praised.

**Implication:** Lead many creatives with appetite/desire, then support with calories/macros/portion facts.

### 2. "Diet food" can still feel indulgent
The "without feeling guilty" theme is strategically important because it attacks a common category tension: healthy eating vs enjoyable food.

**Implication:** Test hooks around crispy/familiar/comfort food that still fits a measured routine.

### 3. Cleanliness and quality are trust assets
Public customer language explicitly mentions cleanliness and high quality.

**Implication:** Behind-the-scenes kitchen, packaging, ingredient and preparation content can support conversion rather than only engagement.

### 4. Trial-to-subscription may be a useful journey
At least one public review describes a meal experience leading directly to membership.

**Implication:** Test conversion content that connects one-off orders/meal trials to subscription value.

## Public brand signal

Current public listings show a strong rating signal around the high-4s with roughly 200+ reviews, but exact rating/review count differs by source and changes over time.

**Rule:** Re-check the live listing before publishing any exact rating/review-count claim.

## What is still missing

This public pass does not tell us enough about:
- why prospects abandon checkout,
- why customers cancel,
- why customers renew,
- delivery objections,
- price objections,
- preferred subscription configuration,
- exact language used in WhatsApp/support.

These require internal VOC and live commercial data.
